What drives satisfaction.

An analysis of 103,904 airline survey responses.

Class and travel purpose matter most

Business class passengers were satisfied far more often than those in Economy, and business travellers far more often than personal travellers. Loyal customers were more satisfied than disloyal ones: 47.7% against 23.7%.

Which services matter

Online boarding has the strongest link to satisfaction, at about 0.50. Inflight entertainment (about 0.40) and seat comfort (about 0.35) follow. Gate location shows almost no link, and departure and arrival time convenience is slightly negative.

Delays matter, but less

The satisfied share falls from 45.8% with no departure delay to about 36% once the delay passes an hour, then levels off. The effect is smaller than the gaps by class or travel purpose.

Model results

Two models were trained on 103,904 passengers and scored on a separate test set of 25,976. Always predicting "not satisfied" would be right about 56.1% of the time. The random forest's most important features were online boarding, inflight wifi, personal travel, economy class and inflight entertainment.

What an airline could do

  • Look at online boarding, inflight entertainment and seat comfort first.
  • Focus on economy and personal travellers, the groups with the lowest satisfaction.
  • Manage delays, but do not expect them to explain most of the gap.
  • Treat gate location and time convenience as lower priorities.

Limits of this analysis

  • The results show association, not cause.
  • Class and travel rates are simple averages and do not control for other factors.
  • The dataset's original source is not documented, so it may not represent any particular airline.
